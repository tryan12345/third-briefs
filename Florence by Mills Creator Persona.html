The pattern

Sales come from creators with 10K or more followers, and the comps simply have far more of them

At every brand in the set, creators under 10K followers make up a large share of the roster but a small share of sales. The difference is volume. Florence by Mills had 482 active creators in the last 90 days. PUMIEY had 3,898 and Comfrt 18,828.

Fashion creators drive almost half of sales. At the comps, everyday creators carry more

Clothing and accessories creators drove 47% of Florence by Mills sales in the last 90 days, against 33% to 34% at Comfrt, Halara and PUMIEY. Creators outside the big categories, such as moms, teachers, nurses and lifestyle accounts, drove 32% to 37% at the comps and 25% at Florence by Mills.

Samples on proven products return far more than samples on new launches

From July to September, about 282 samples on hero products returned $128.6K in sales. About 199 samples on new launches returned $2.4K. The 2027 plan sends 60% of samples to heroes and new colors on proven shapes, and 40% to launches.

Archetypes

Priority 1

A1. The everyday mom and home creator

Creator level not available. 20K to 250K followers. Home, family, holiday and day-in-the-life content.

Buyer trigger served

Comfort and the holiday, gifting and matching moment.

Format

Product worn through a real day: decorating, school runs, the evening wind-down. Longer organic videos work here.

Proven in market

One creator's holiday decorating series sold $4.9K of Daze Off in three organic videos of 3 to 4 minutes. A nurse creator sold $4.1K with short matching pajama videos.

Priority 1

A2. The deals and finds curator

Creator level not available. 20K to 100K followers. Posts daily across many brands.

Buyer trigger served

More colors and prints, and value for the price.

Format

A haul of every print or color in 30 to 50 seconds, with the price and a clear favorite.

Proven in market

The top video in the window showed nine holiday prints and sold 342 units. The same creator type tops the comps: a single finds creator sold 1,903 units of a Comfrt crew in a 10-second video.

Priority 1

A3. The real-size try-on creator

Creator level not available. 10K to 350K followers. Shows sizes from XS to 2XL and audiences from 18 to 54.

Buyer trigger served

Fit confidence. This answers the number one buyer question: what size to get.

Format

Size, height and usual size said in the first seconds, then a turn and a stretch test. Daylight for light colors.

Proven in market

A long sleeve creator with an audience mostly aged 35 to 54 sold $6.4K for Florence by Mills. PUMIEY's top long sleeve videos follow this format and convert at 1.56 units per 1,000 views.

Priority 2

A4. The wellness and lounge creator

Creator level not available. 10K to 50K followers. Yoga, self-care and home routine content.

Buyer trigger served

Softness and stretch for wearing all day.

Format

Short, calm clips of the product in use: stretching, lounging, getting ready for bed.

Recruiting note

A home and wellness creator with 20K followers sold 234 Daze Off sets from one 9-second organic video. Few are on the roster today.

Priority 2

A5. The fashion stylist

Creator level not available. 20K to 250K followers. Outfit and styling content.

Buyer trigger served

A staple that goes with everything.

Format

Three ways to wear one piece, styled with jeans or a skirt.

Recruiting note

The core of today's roster. Keep them, and grow A1 to A4 around them.

Recruit for range, then brief every creator on fit

  • Send hero basics and Daze Off to A1, A3 and A4 creators first, with a target of more of them each month.
  • Recruit across sizes and ages on purpose, using the shades and sizes a creator shows on camera.
  • Give every creator an Informed Creative Brief that asks for the size worn, their height and a stretch test.
  • Approach the 35 strong matches, starting with the 7 in the top 25.
  • Keep answering good creator requests within two days.
  • Track creators who sell $10K or more in 90 days as the main measure of roster depth.
