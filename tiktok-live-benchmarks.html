Livestreaming 101

Reading TikTok Shop LIVE data

What the LIVE dashboard is comparing you against, what the numbers mean, and the category benchmarks for Beauty & Personal Care and Toys & Hobbies.

01

Two comparisons on every metric

Every metric on the LIVE dashboard has two lines under it. One compares the LIVE to your last 30 days. The other compares it to the category benchmark.

TikTok shows these as percent changes. It doesn't show the benchmark number itself. You can back it out with a standard percent change:

Benchmark = your value ÷ (1 + % above)
Benchmark = your value ÷ (1 − % below)

We checked this across two Toys & Hobbies LIVEs and two Beauty LIVEs. Every metric landed on the same benchmark within rounding.

02

What the benchmark is

The benchmark is the average performance of top-performing LIVEs in your category. TikTok picks those streams for strong audience appeal and strong conversion. It doesn't pick them for size.

That makes it a high bar on rates and a low bar on volume. The average benchmark LIVE gets about 15,000 impressions. A brand stream with real feed distribution can get hundreds of thousands or millions.

  • You pick the category level in the dashboard. Level 1 is the broadest. You can go down to the leaf category for a closer comparison.
  • The benchmark is the same for every LIVE you look up. A LIVE from early in the month gets the same benchmark as one from last week.
  • Each benchmark is averaged on its own. Don't divide one benchmark by another. Benchmark views ÷ benchmark impressions gives 42%, while the tap-through benchmark is about 158%.

03

What "last 30 days" is

This is your own account. It's the only comparison against yourself on the page.

The window appears to be tied to when the dashboard last updated, not to the date of the LIVE. Two LIVEs from the same account, run 20 days apart, backed out to the same 30-day numbers. So an older LIVE can be compared against streams that happened after it.

The "Needs attention" flag only fires on the last-30-day comparison. A metric can be far above benchmark and still get flagged.

04

How each metric is calculated

Every rate ties directly to the raw counts on the dashboard.

MetricFormula
Tap-through rateViews ÷ impressions
Product exposure rateProduct impressions ÷ views
CTRProduct clicks ÷ product impressions
LIVE CTRProduct clicks ÷ views
CTOROrders ÷ product clicks
Show GPMGMV ÷ impressions × 1,000
>1m LIVE viewing rate>1m views ÷ views
Total attributed GMVGMV for the full LIVE
GMV per hourTotal GMV ÷ stream length

Impressions are times the LIVE showed in someone's feed. Product impressions are times a product card showed to someone already in the LIVE. CTR and tap-through use different denominators, so they don't chain together.

06

How a percent gets over 100

Say 10 kids come to a birthday party. If 5 kids each get a cookie, that's 50%. If all 10 get one, that's 100%.

Now say every kid eats 3 cookies. That's 30 cookies for 10 kids, or 300%. The percent goes over 100 because each kid had more than one.

Like rate works the same way. It's likes divided by viewers, and people can tap the heart as many times as they want. A 26,650% like rate means the average viewer tapped like about 266 times.

BenchmarkWhat it means
Like rate ~26,650%About 266 likes per viewer
Comment rate ~312%About 3 comments per viewer
Product exposure rate ~405%Each viewer saw product cards about 4 times
Tap-through rate ~169%More views than feed impressions. Viewers also come in from the Following tab, notifications and profiles.

Benchmark streams are small LIVEs with loyal fans, so these rates run high. A big stream full of people scrolling past might get 1.5 likes per viewer, or 150%.

07

How to use it

  • Compare volume to the benchmark. Use impressions per hour and GMV per hour, since totals don't account for stream length.
  • Compare rates to your own last 30 days. Benchmark rates come from small, warm audiences. If your LIVE got real feed reach, most of those rates will show red.
  • Narrow the category level when you want a closer peer group.
  • Retention and CTOR can make up for lost reach. The example below shows it.
