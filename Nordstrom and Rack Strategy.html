Nordstrom and Nordstrom Rack on TikTok Shop

What it would take to build, launch, run, and scale both businesses on TikTok Shop, and why the two should not run the same playbook.

The short version

Nordstrom and Nordstrom Rack are not on TikTok Shop. No US department store or major off-price retailer is. We checked Macy's, Saks, TJ Maxx, Ross, and Burlington and found no shops. These retailers are still paying for reach on TikTok. Marshalls ran a paid creator post that hit 26.4 million plays in August. Nordstrom's paid creator videos hit 25.4 million plays in the last 90 days. None of it had a product link, so none of those viewers could buy.

We think Rack goes first. Rack carries hundreds of brands people already want. TikTok is where shoppers discover what to buy. A Rack shop puts those brands in front of them, in one place, at Rack prices. Most of the Rack shop is a steady supply of Nordstrom Made brands that do not turn over quickly. Limited-inventory designer drops sit on top of that core and give the shop its urgency. We also found about 3,960 creators already selling $25,000 or more a month with videos that look like discount-store finds and deal hauls.

Nordstrom launches within a quarter of Rack, as its own shop with its own job. Its job is the current season at full price, on the Nordstrom.com calendar. Trend and style creators bring it new customers. We are not counting on Rack buyers to trade up to fill it.

Bullseye Deals is the closest model for Rack. It sells name-brand overstock, mostly through live. It did $9.1 million in the last 90 days and about $3.5 million in the last 28. Its own handle went live 93 times in the last 28 days, about 3 a day, and posted no videos. Each live is a named show, like Morning Markdowns, Kids Korner, or The Night Cap.

The two shops share brands. They run different playbooks. Rack is built around a weekly drop live and a cohort of Rack Finds creators. Nordstrom is built around creators, and it only goes live at Nordstrom.com events. Creators make most of the content for both.

The creators Nordstrom pays today do not sell on TikTok Shop. We looked at the ten biggest #NordstromPartner creators from the last 90 days. In the last 30 days they sold between $0 and $5,206 on the platform. We have to build a new creator base from people who already sell apparel there.

If each shop takes 0.5% of the categories it sells in, Rack does about $3.0 million a month in GMV and Nordstrom does about $1.1 million. Rack would land between Tillys and Edikted. Nordstrom would land at about half of Tillys.

The plan follows seven parts: brand role and opportunity, merchandising, affiliates and creators, live commerce, creator live, campaigns and moments, and the operating model and roadmap. Nordstrom and Rack are compared side by side throughout.

Nordstrom should lean into curation, newness, and owned brands

What Nordstrom has on TikTok Shop is its edit. BP., Topshop, Zella, Open Edit, and Treasure & Bond have no shop on the platform. Nordstrom is one of Topshop's global wholesale partners, and Topshop has no US TikTok Shop either. We want the Nordstrom shop to be where a shopper sees the new season first.

The @nordstrom account has 227,017 followers. 61% of its audience is 35 or older and 7% is 18 to 24. The account has an engagement rate of 0.5% and has never gone live. Nordstrom's own account will not bring in many new customers. Trend and style creators will.

Rack should lean into value, discovery, and scarcity

Rack's advantage is the brands. Shoppers already buy fashion on TikTok Shop in volume. Cider did $13.7 million in the last 90 days and Halara did $26.5 million. Both sell their own labels. Rack brings the brand names people know and want, at Rack prices, to the place they discover what to buy.

The Rack shop runs on two layers. The core is a consistent volume of Nordstrom Made product: BP., Zella, Open Edit, and Treasure & Bond. That inventory is steady, so creators can post about it for weeks and paid media can scale it. The second layer is limited-inventory designer drops. Those pieces are few and often one of a kind, which is what drops, price reveals, countdowns, and auctions need.

Rack should launch first

What we would do
Launch the Rack shop first. Launch the Nordstrom shop within one quarter.
Why it makes sense
Rack carries brands people already want, at prices that feel like a find. TikTok is where shoppers discover product. Rack would be the first major off-price retailer on the platform. The pool of deal and haul creators is large. Nordstrom Made gives Rack steady inventory from day one, and designer drops give it urgency.
How it works
Rack builds the operating base first: shop setup, fulfillment, creator recruiting, and live production. Nordstrom launches on that base with a separate assortment, creator cohort, and event calendar.
In practice
Rack opens with a curated catalog and one weekly drop live. Nordstrom opens with a current-season edit timed to a Nordstrom.com launch, with creator samples shipped 2 to 6 weeks before.

Starting with Rack has a real risk. Someone who meets Nordstrom through a markdown may never pay full price. We handle that two ways. The Nordstrom shop goes after its own new customers from day one. Section 2 sets rules that keep the two shops apart.

A VIP cohort for each shop

What we would do
Build a VIP cohort of 10 to 15 creators for Nordstrom and a Rack Finds cohort of 15 to 25 creators for Rack.
Why it makes sense
A small group of proven sellers posts every week and carries launches. A smaller cohort is easier to manage and keeps the higher rate focused on creators who sell.
How it works
Creators qualify on GMV and posting rate in the first 60 days. Nordstrom VIPs get launch samples through TikTok Shop's sample flow and the 20% rate. Rack Finds creators get priority sample approval on each drop and the 20% rate.
In practice
Each Thursday Rack drop has 15 or more Rack Finds videos posting the same week. Each Nordstrom season launch has 10 or more VIP videos posting on launch day.

Rack cadence builds in stages

What we would do
Start Rack with one weekly drop live plus tentpole mega lives. Add sessions only when they clear our GPM goal.
Why it makes sense
Every live session costs host time and production. Edikted's biggest sessions were named events. A fixed weekly slot trains shoppers to return.
How it works
One slot a week at a fixed day and time, 4 hours long. Each week's new arrivals are held for it. A second slot is added when the last four sessions average above the $6 GPM goal. A slot is dropped if it averages below the $4 floor for four weeks.
In practice
"Rack Drop Thursday" from 6 to 10 p.m. Eastern: 100 to 200 new-to-Rack items, price reveals every 10 minutes, and a flash deal at each hour.

Rack creator storefronts

What we would do
Pilot 5 to 10 creators who run a recurring "Rack storefront" live on their own handle, selling each week's drop.
Why it makes sense
Creator live multiplies Rack's live hours without adding brand production. Each creator brings an audience that already buys clothing in live.
How it works
Each creator gets curated inventory from the weekly drop, a 20% commission, and a shared promotion calendar. Rack supports each storefront with Shop Ads on the creator's live.
In practice
Five creators each go live once a week on a different day. Rack has a storefront live four or five nights a week on top of its own Thursday drop.

Launch approach

What we would do
Launch Rack first. Launch Nordstrom within one quarter on a Nordstrom.com launch date.
Why it makes sense
Rack brings brands shoppers want to the place they discover product, and its inventory fits live and drops. Nordstrom needs its own launch moment and pre-seeded creator content.
How it works
Rack runs cold start, then adds live and Rack Finds. Nordstrom seeds VIP creators 2 to 6 weeks before its launch date.
In practice
Rack opens with its first Rack Drop Thursday. Nordstrom opens with a season launch live hosted by a stylist.

Top strategic bets to test

  2. Rack Finds. Will a cohort of 15 to 25 creators stay active and selling at a 20% rate?

  4. Auctions at tentpoles. Do $1-start auctions work for handbags and one-off apparel in new condition?
  5. Stylist-hosted Nordstrom live. Can a Nordstrom stylist hold an event live above the $6 GPM goal?
  6. Topshop at Nordstrom. Is the Topshop return big enough to carry a launch on TikTok Shop?

Risks

We have no data yet that Rack buyers move to full price. The Nordstrom plan does not depend on it.

Nordstrom Made sells in both shops. If current-season product leaks into Rack, Rack will pull buyers away from full price.

Nordstrom's current paid creators do not sell on the platform. Both shops start their commerce creator base from zero.

Every live session costs host time and production. We only add sessions after they clear the GPM gate.

We have not confirmed that TikTok allows auctions for new-condition and marked-down apparel. We will check with TikTok before we build the auction handle.

Auction sales mostly show up outside live attribution. We will need Seller Center reporting to measure them.

Shoes and sized apparel get returned more. Returns cut net revenue against gross GMV.

Designer drop inventory sells through fast. If the product feed falls behind, creator videos will point to items that are sold out.

Some exclusives made for Nordstrom by other brands may not allow TikTok Shop listings.

We are still validating cold start day counts. We will confirm them before quoting dates.
