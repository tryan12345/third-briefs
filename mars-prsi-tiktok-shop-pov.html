PRSI Measurement on TikTok Shop

Our read on which PRSI KPIs work on TikTok Shop, and what the framework still needs before it can measure a shoppable channel.

Where we land

Four answers before the detail

We read the framework against what TikTok Shop Seller Center actually reports. Here is the short version of our POV on each of your four questions.

1. About half the set ports.

The reaction and engagement metrics carry over well. Anything denominated in reach or followers does not, because Seller Center reports neither. The earned media inputs like outlet inclusion and headline inclusion have no TikTok equivalent.

2. The PRSI Quality Score cannot be calculated from Seller Center.

Five of its inputs have no source on TikTok. Its denominators do not exist in the platform. Run it on what is left and the output cannot be compared to a PR score, even though it will look like it can.

3. TikTok Shop already reports the metric you are arguing for.

GPM is gross revenue per 1,000 views. The "320 impressions to sell one pack of M&Ms" figure on slides 16 and 19 is that same ratio, inverted.

4. The framework has no slot for the things social commerce runs on.

Sample seeding yield, GMV decay, new to brand share, LIVE watch time, and the ad assisted split are all missing. Some of them connect the executional layer straight to your business outcomes.

01 / The structural difference

Why the framework needs different treatment here

PRSI measurement exists to solve a proof problem. Earned coverage produces an impression and then the trail goes cold. The reach deflators on slides 17 and 18, the quality composite, and the virality multiplier all exist to reconstruct what happened after that impression.

TikTok Shop does not have that problem. The view, the product click, the order, the delivery, and the refund all carry the same video ID. The loop closes inside one system, at the level of the individual video.

That changes what measurement is for here. The useful question on TikTok Shop is what an exposure was worth per unit of attention.

One practical consequence for your roll-up

Do not run TikTok Shop views through the comprehensive exposure deflation model. That model is right for online news and podcast listenership, where a UVM figure badly overstates readership. Applied to TikTok Shop it discounts the one channel that can already prove the sale, and it converts a hard number into a modeled one.

Report TikTok Shop in its own metrics. Reconcile with the rest of PESO at the GMV line inside MMM, not at the reach line.

03 / Question two

Why the Quality Score does not calculate, and what replaces it

You flagged this one yourselves, and the concern is right. Three things break when the score meets Seller Center.

The inputs do not exist

Earned Key Message Pull Through, Brand Headline Inclusion, Target Outlet Inclusion, PR Net Sentiment, and LLM Narrative Alignment are all earned media constructs. TikTok Shop has no outlets, no headlines, and no press pickup. Five of the score's inputs have no source, and any proxy would be invented.

The denominators do not exist

Organic Engagement Rate and the reaction inputs are denominated in reach or followers. Seller Center reports views and does not carry follower counts on video rows. Every ratio in the quality set needs a different bottom half before it can be computed.

The index stops being comparable

A composite indexed to 100 needs stable benchmarks on every input. Run it on a partial input set and a 100 from TikTok Shop means something different from a 100 on a PR program. Rolled together in a Snacking level report, the two would misstate the total, and nobody reading the roll-up would be able to spot it.

Where the fix comes from

The framework's own definition of quality is the thing blocking you. TikTok Shop attaches the conversion event to the impression, which no other PESO channel does. Define quality by what the impression did and the score becomes calculable.

Proposed: Commerce Quality Score

Same shape as the PRSI Quality Score so it sits alongside it in reporting. Indexed to 100, benchmarks met or missed, over 100 means you beat expectation. Different inputs. Every one of them available in Seller Center with no additional instrumentation.

InputCalculationWhat it answers
Product CTRProduct clicks / video viewsDid the impression create intent
Click to order rateOrders / product clicksDid the intent survive the product page
GPM(GMV / views) x 1,000What the attention was worth
Watch retentionAverage watch time / video length. Average watch time for LIVE.Did anyone stay
Sale durability1 minus refund rate, read with post purchase ratingDid the sale hold

Method

  • Index each input against a trailing 90 day benchmark for that shop and that category. Equal weight across the five.
  • Compute at video level, then weight by views to reach a campaign figure. Averaging video level scores unweighted will let a 400 view test post move the campaign number.
  • Refresh benchmarks quarterly. Category and price point move these figures more than creative does, and a benchmark set in Q1 will not survive Q4 seasonality.

Two inputs need a caveat. Refund rate lags the sale by weeks, so sale durability should be reported on a trailing basis and the campaign score restated once. Watch retention is reported differently for in feed video and LIVE, so the two should be benchmarked separately rather than pooled.

04 / Question three

Our POV on the calculations, and how to simplify

The executional and diagnostic sets carry more than twenty metrics between them. That is defensible for PR, where no single number closes the loop and each metric covers a gap the others cannot. On TikTok Shop most of these metrics decompose into one another. Reporting all of them means reporting the same fact several times.

The simplification comes out of an arithmetic identity.

GPM = Product CTR  x  Click to order rate  x  AOV  x  1,000 Views cancel out. Revenue per 1,000 views is the product of the three levers that produce it, so the headline number carries its own diagnostic layer.

When GPM falls you already know where to look. Low product CTR is a hook and tagging problem. Low click to order is a listing, price, or review problem. Falling AOV is a bundle or SKU mix problem. No extra reporting is required to run that diagnosis, because the three components come out of the same Seller Center export as the headline.

Four numbers per campaign

This is what we would put in front of a Snacking measurement lead, and everything else sits underneath it as diagnostic.

Scale
Shoppable Views
Views on product tagged video and LIVE, split organic and ad delivered. Labeled as impressions, never as reach.
Quality
GPM
Gross revenue per 1,000 views. Your quality impressions metric. Native to the platform and comparable across creators and formats.
Reaction
Non Affiliate Content Share
Views on product tagged content from creators we did not commission, over total product tagged views. The virality multiplier, rebuilt for this platform.
Outcome
Attributed GMV
Split by surface. Video, LIVE, product card, and affiliate against shop owned.

These map onto your three jobs to be done, with one addition. How many people saw it is Shoppable Views. Was it any good is GPM. Did anyone care is Non Affiliate Content Share. The fourth question is whether it sold, and TikTok Shop answers that one in the same report.

05 / Question four

What social commerce needs that the framework does not have

Grouped by the job each one does. All of these are reportable from Seller Center or from the affiliate and sample modules alongside it.

Conversion core

  • GPM. Covered above. This is the single most important addition.
  • Attributed GMV by surface. Video, LIVE, product card, and shop owned against affiliate. These have different economics and blending them hides problems. On one managed account we found a blended GMV Max return of 0.96 while product cards inside the same account returned 4.07, because ninety percent of budget sat in video ads. Nothing in the blended figure showed that.
  • Ad assisted against organic split. Without it, earned and shared tactics get credited with sales that paid delivery produced. This matters most for the Earned and Shared columns in your PESO definitions, where boosted creator content sits close to the line.

Creator and seeding economics

  • Sample to post rate. You have product gifting written into Earned Creator Endorsement in the PESO definitions. The gifting program has a yield, and nothing in the current framework measures it. Samples requested against samples that produced a post is the first number.
  • Sample cost per posting creator, and sample cost per dollar of GMV. This is the ROI of the earned creator tactic, and it is the number that decides whether seeding scales next year.
  • GMV concentration. Share of affiliate GMV coming from the top ten percent of creators. High concentration means the program is one creator relationship away from a bad quarter, and no other metric in the set will surface that.

Time and durability

  • GMV decay curve. Shop videos keep selling for weeks after posting. A campaign window built for a PR burst will close before the revenue arrives. Report GMV at 7, 30, and 60 days from post.
  • Return and refund rate by creator. A creator who oversells the product generates GMV that reverses. Without this, the highest performing creator in a campaign report can be the one costing the most money.

Penetration

  • New to brand share. Seller Center reports new against returning customers at shop level. Penetration is one of the three business outcomes at the top of your hierarchy, and TikTok Shop is the only place in PESO where Mars can observe it directly instead of modeling it. That is a direct read on a business outcome from an executional metric.

LIVE

  • Average watch time, GMV per hour, product click rate during LIVE, and peak concurrent viewers. The framework currently has no LIVE metrics at all. Social Commerce and Live Shopping are named under Shared in your PESO definitions, so the tactic is already in scope.

06 / Data governance

Three things to settle before the first roll-up

Name one system of record

Seller Center, platform native analytics, and third party estimators will each return a different GMV for the same window. We recommend Seller Center as the book of record for owned shops, with third party tools used for competitive context only. Attribution in third party tools lags on recent windows and converges with older ones, so a figure pulled on Monday for last week will not match the same figure pulled a month later.

Discount third party efficiency estimates

We have tested the widely used third party TikTok Shop estimators against Seller Center figures. They overstate ad efficiency by roughly 2.6x to 3.8x, because they capture around sixty percent of video attributed ad revenue and miss product card revenue entirely. Anyone pulling a competitive ROAS figure into a Mars deck should divide it by about three before treating it as real. Competitive lookbacks are also capped, commonly at 180 days, which constrains any year over year competitive read.

Treat TikTok Shop GMV as a floor

In app attribution is last click within a short window. It cannot see the TikTok video that sent someone to a Target aisle or a Walmart basket, which for Snacking is most of the commercial effect. TikTok Shop GMV understates the total commercial effect, and the halo belongs in MMM. Slide 14 makes the same argument about plugging the framework in.

07 / Next

What we would need to build this out

  • Seller Center report access or scheduled exports for the Snacking shops in scope, including the affiliate and sample modules.
  • Confirmation of which shops are live and which are in test, so benchmarks are not set on ramp period data.
  • A decision on the benchmark window for indexing. We suggest trailing 90 days per shop and category, refreshed quarterly.
  • A decision on system of record before the first cross channel roll-up rather than after it.

With those in place we can produce a worked Commerce Quality Score against a live Snacking shop and a sample campaign report in the four number format, so your measurement leads can see the output before committing the framework to it.
