Loops Lab

Turning views into GMV. Prepared by Third for Loops Lab, September 15, 2026. Covers October 1 to December 29, 2026. Benchmarks of $15,000, $25,000 and $40,000 against a 2.0x return on paid.

The headline

Loops Lab content converts

When someone watches a Loops Lab video, they buy at a rate that beats the closest comparable product on TikTok Shop.

$67.70

GMV earned per 1,000 views on Loops Lab videos that convert. Stickerbox, a $137.98 kids tech toy launched nine months earlier, earns $24.06 on the same measure.

Across the full launch library of 183 videos the figure is $17.03 per 1,000 views. Conversion is not the variable. Total audience is: the shop has reached 68,479 views since launching in July.

A single Stickerbox video reached 826,500 views. That is the scale of audience available in this category.

This plan does one thing. It scales the audience, raising average views per video and the volume of creator content behind the product. The conversion engine underneath is already performing.

4. The sampling plan

The sample engine is working and the plan runs on half the samples

117 approved samples produced 183 videos, which is 1.56 videos per approved sample and ahead of the house benchmark of about 1.5. Creators receiving Loops Lab samples post reliably.

Sample volume runs at half the original proposal. The content volume comes back through written minimums instead. Contracted creators already deliver between 2 and 6 pieces from a single sample, so moving the average to 3.0 more than recovers the difference.

What changes

  • Outreach lists are filtered on platform GMV before follower count. Minimum $25,000 trailing 30 days, which is Third's L3 tier.
  • Every creator is scored on floor views, the lowest view count across their recent in-category videos. Not their best video and not their average, both of which hide a single lucky post.
  • Toys and Hobbies category creators are weighted heavily. They carry 12 of the top 15 priority creators.
  • The four creators who have already sold Stickerbox go out first, in week 1.
  • Every sample from month 2 onward carries a written content minimum of 3 pieces.
  • Refundable samples get tested, where the creator buys and is reimbursed after posting. The shop currently runs none and it would cut cash exposure materially.

5. Ninety days, week by week

Weeks 1 to 4

Fix the catalog and restart outreach

  • Consolidate the two Starter Set listings into one. Two listings at $122.30 and $139.00 sitting in different categories split creator attribution and compete in search.
  • Decide whether the hero sits in Educational Toys or Musical Instruments and Accessories. The second grew 15.6% month over month in August against 2.6% for the first.
  • Send samples to @keri_danielle, @michaela_motherhood, @kenzie.mikel and @cassieaverytts in week 1.
  • Work the priority creator shortlist in order. Target 75 approved samples in the month.
  • Deploy $5,580 of paid media across creatives with proven conversion, held to a $45 cost per order ceiling.
  • Every sample ships with a creative brief built from the hooks that have already produced sales on this product.
Weeks 5 to 8

Buy reach deliberately

  • Seed 7 to 10 creators above 100,000 followers and treat the cost as distribution rather than affiliate commission.
  • Target 200 approved samples in the month, all on written content minimums.
  • Scale paid only against creatives already under $45 per order. Q4 demand should carry seeding on its own from here.
  • Stop any creative drifting above $45 per order within 48 hours. Concentration beats spread at this budget level.
  • Review the age of children appearing in content. The listing says 8 and up and much of the converting content shows preschoolers.
Weeks 9 to 13

Scale what cleared

  • Target 250 approved samples in the month, all on content minimums.
  • Open GMV Max once the consolidated hero listing clears 50 videos and the shop clears 600, which this ramp reaches in month 2.
  • Hold the $45 cost per order ceiling and scale budget only against creatives already under it.
  • Re-engage every creator from months 1 and 2 who posted and earned views. They are already briefed and are the most efficient volume available.

Cold start timelines in this section are Third house assumptions and are under active review.

What we need from Loops Lab

  • A decision on catalog consolidation. One listing, one price, one category.
  • Landed cost of goods on the Starter Set. At retail, 525 samples is $72,975 and that is not the right number to plan against.
  • A decision on refundable samples, where the creator buys and is reimbursed after posting. It would cut cash exposure and the shop runs none today.
  • Monthly sample allocation confirmed against the 75, 200, 250 ramp, and agreement that samples from month 2 carry a written content minimum of 3 pieces.
  • A month one paid media budget of $5,580, held to a $45 cost per order ceiling.
  • A clear answer on the intended age range. If the product is genuinely for 8 to 13, the casting in creator content has to change.
  • Expansion band roadmap, including which are shipping and when, so creators can speak to availability accurately.
  • Permission to re-approach @keri_danielle and @michaela_motherhood directly, including on commercial terms if a flat fee is what closes them.

Risks worth naming

Tonies owns this price band with licensed characters. Loops Lab has no character recognition to trade on and that gap does not close with better creative. Stickerbox shows a product without licensed IP can win here, and it took nine months and 494 creators to get there.

The product needs an app to work and is sold on screen-free play. This is the objection most likely to stop the parent who came in on the strongest reason to buy, and it cannot be edited out of the content.

Sample cost at this ramp is a real cash commitment and the plan does not yet carry confirmed cost of goods.

The month one benchmark of $15,000 depends on paid media performing at 2.0x. Four boosted creatives have already delivered orders inside the required cost per order, so the efficiency is evidenced, but month one carries more risk than months 2 and 3 combined because it runs before the content library and Q4 demand are in place.

Months 2 and 3 lean on Q4 gifting demand, modelled at a 25% and 45% uplift. Those multipliers are an estimate rather than a figure pulled from last year's category data. Strip the uplift out and month 2 lands at $20,400 against a $25,000 benchmark.

The move from 1.56 to 3.0 videos per sample is the single assumption carrying the reduced sample budget. It depends on content minimums being agreed in writing at the point of sampling. Without them the plan delivers roughly half of what is shown.

The $67.70 figure rests on 8 converting videos and 18,676 views. It is real and it is an early sample, and it should firm up as volume builds.

Consolidating the two listings will reset review count and sales history on whichever one is retired.
