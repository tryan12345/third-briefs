02 · The archetypes

Three creator types sell this machine

Archetype A

The Home Demonstrator

5K – 50K followers

Films in her own kitchen. Shows the full make: milk in, flakes in, whisk running, slow pour. Talks about texture and temperature. Posts the same product weekly for months.

Examples: @shopjoelle, @megansue100, @goodnesshavens
Why she works: the pour shot is the conversion moment, and she reshoots it endlessly.
Recruit: heaviest volume. This is 60% of the target roster.
Archetype B

The Deal Announcer

40K – 300K followers

Leads with the discount, not the product. Short videos, stacked coupon language, hard urgency. Converts on price events rather than on desire.

Examples: @promocodepal, @nateshopstealz, @daddydealesthome
Why she works: owns Black Friday. Highest conversion rates in the dataset sit here.
Recruit: activate against price events only. Dead weight outside a promo window.
Archetype C

The LIVE Seller

20K – 60K followers

Runs long sessions with the machine on the desk, brewing on camera. @roxtalksshow ran 121 lives against this product. @flowersnbirdies ran 84. This is the archetype the comp set is built on and the one this shop is shortest of.

Examples: @roxtalksshow, @flowersnbirdies
Why she works: the objection about heat and portion size gets answered live, in real time.
Recruit: highest priority. Nine of the shop’s ten best-ever lives belong to one creator. LIVE produced $54.99 in the last 99 days.

Archetype A drives the base. Archetype B drives the spike. Archetype C is the only one that can answer objections in real time on a $212 appliance, and it is currently the emptiest lane on the shop.

Two pieces of evidence say Archetype C should be recruited first rather than last. October 2025 was the shop’s cold-start month and live carried 63% of its GMV. And on Ninja CREAMi XL, the closest comp, there are 2,450 linked lives against 1,733 linked videos. More lives than videos on the hero SKU.

27,575 DMs produced 169 replies across the same window. That is a 0.61% reply rate. The offer is not landing, and the likely reason is that it reads as a chocolate offer to creators whose audience buys appliances.

The approval gate is the second leak. Three quarters of sample requests are declined. On a $212 unit that caution is understandable, but it caps the roster at a size that cannot produce 50 videos a week.

Target profile for recruitment

  • 5K to 50K followers. 151 of the current 227 sit here and they carry the revenue.
  • Home, kitchen, appliance or deal-finder lane. Not food. Not chocolate.
  • History of repeat posting on one SKU. Look for 30+ videos on a single product in their history.
  • Already posts other countertop appliances. CREAMi, SLUSHi, SodaStream, espresso machines.
  • Shoots in a real kitchen. The pour shot needs a counter, not a desk.

Fixes to run first

  • Rewrite the DM around the appliance. Lead with the machine and the $212 price point, not the chocolate.
  • Move to refundable samples. Creator buys, gets refunded on post. Protects unit economics and lets the roster scale.
  • Raise the approval rate. 25% cannot support the content volume the season requires.
  • Build a LIVE roster. Two creators account for essentially all live activity on this product.
  • Recruit Spanish-language creators. Two July videos in Spanish converted at 12.6% and 15.2% with no program behind them.
